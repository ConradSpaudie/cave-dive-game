# Logbook Species List

> **Status: PROPOSAL for Conrad to approve.** Nothing in this note is decided yet. Once approved, move the agreed parts into [[Creatures, Flora and Logbook]] and log the decision in [[Decision Log]]. Depth zones also answer the open question in [[Cave Generation]] ("How depth zones are defined and what changes between them").

This note proposes depth zones and a starter list of 50 loggable species (32 grounded in real cave and deep-water life, 18 invented for the deep zones, each invented one still based on a real animal or plant). Sizes are game measurement ranges, close to real life for real species. Depths are game meters, not realistic dive depths.

## Legend
- **Behavior:** Harmless, Skittish (flees, hard to measure), Territorial (warns, then attacks if you stay), Dangerous (attacks or hurts on contact).
- **Glows:** No, or Yes with a lantern brightness from 1 (faint) to 5 (lights a whole chamber). "Not catchable" means it glows but cannot go in the lantern.
- **Rarity:** Common, Uncommon, Rare, Very Rare, Legendary.

---

## Proposed depth zones

| # | Zone | Depth (game m) | Mood | Light level | What changes |
|---|------|----------------|------|-------------|--------------|
| 1 | **The Mouth** | 0 to 20 | Bright, calm, a tutorial that doesn't feel like one | Sunlight shafts, green and blue water | Familiar life, open water, easy logging. Roots and ferns at the entrance. Teaches the measuring tool. |
| 2 | **Twilight Throat** | 20 to 60 | Curious, a little eerie | Light drops off sharply around corners; flashlight needed | First pale and eyeless animals. First dry chambers with glowworms. First silt rooms. First glowing fungi to catch. |
| 3 | **The Black Galleries** | 60 to 120 | Quiet, lonely, tense | Pitch black except your flashlight and rare glowers | True cave specialists (olm, remipedes). First real hazard (electric eels). Flashlight batteries start running low, so the lantern matters. |
| 4 | **The Sulfur Deep** | 120 to 200 | Wrong, toxic, sickly yellow and white | Black, with a few bright bioluminescent pockets | A cloudy sulfide layer (halocline) you dive through. Chemosynthetic life (it eats chemicals, not sunlight). Invented creatures start appearing. Lure predators. Acid drips in air pockets. |
| 5 | **The Old Water** | 200+ (the "endless" part) | Ancient, huge, awe plus 15% horror | Bioluminescence is the only light | Prehistoric giants and living fossils. Big open caverns after tight squeezes. Fossil beds. The rarest lantern creatures. The ending lives somewhere down here. |

**Zone transitions should be felt, not read:** a temperature shift on the watch, a visible halocline shimmer between zones 3 and 4, water color change, and new ambient sounds (see [[Audio]]).

## Zone 1: The Mouth

| Name | Type | Real-world inspiration | Size range | Behavior | Glows? | Rarity | Memorable moment |
|------|------|------------------------|------------|----------|--------|--------|------------------|
| Stalag Moss | Moss | *Eucladium verticillatum*, which colonizes lit stalagmites in show caves | Patch 5 to 40 cm | Harmless | No | Common | The first thing everyone logs. Someone measures it with the tape upside down and argues it is 40 cm "if you count the rock." |
| Maidenhair Spleenwort | Fern | *Asplenium trichomanes*, common at cave entrances | 5 to 20 cm | Harmless | No | Common | Grows on the ledge where you tie the surface stake. Easy first entry. |
| Green Rock Film | Algae | Lampenflora algae films on wet, lit cave rock | Patch 10 cm to 2 m | Harmless | No | Common | Slippery. Climbing out on it makes you slide back into the water in front of everyone. |
| Goblin's Gold | Moss | *Schistostega pennata*, a moss whose lens-shaped cells reflect light as a gold-green shine | Patch 2 to 10 cm | Harmless | Shines only when your light hits it (not catchable) | Uncommon | Players think it is treasure and try to dig it up. It is worth nothing. It goes dull the moment you pick it. |
| Root Curtain | Plant | Tree roots that hang down into Yucatan cenotes to reach water | Strand 0.5 to 6 m | Harmless | No | Common | The rope tangles in it constantly. Classic "who wrapped us around the tree" moment. |
| Cenote Molly | Fish | *Poecilia mexicana*, livebearing fish of Mexican cenotes and sulfur springs | 4 to 9 cm | Skittish (schools scatter) | No | Common | A whole school bolts at once and blows silt into the face of whoever is behind. |
| Silver Tetra (sighted form) | Fish | *Astyanax mexicanus* surface form, the ancestor of the blind cavefish | 6 to 12 cm | Skittish | No | Common | Pairs with the blind form in Zone 2. Logging both unlocks a logbook note: "Same fish?" |
| Entrance Crayfish | Crustacean | Surface crayfish (*Procambarus*) that wander into cave mouths | 6 to 12 cm | Territorial (snaps at fingers) | No | Common | Pinches a glove and hangs on. Player swims around waving a crayfish. |
| Cave Cricket | Insect | Camel crickets (*Ceuthophilus*) in dry cave zones | 1.5 to 4 cm body, legs much longer | Skittish (jumps at your face) | No | Common | Dry chamber only. Fifty of them jump at once when you turn on your light. Pure jump scare, pure comedy. |
| Roost Bats | Mammal | Cave bats in entrance chambers | Wingspan 20 to 35 cm | Skittish | No | Uncommon | Logged by measuring one that hangs still. Shouting on proximity chat makes the whole roost explode outward. Guano floor is slippery. |

| Sulfur Bloom Mat | Bacteria | White bacterial mats (like *Beggiatoa*) on cave floors, like wet cloth or slime | Patch 20 cm to 5 m | Harmless, but kicks up a huge cloud when touched | No | Common | Fins brush it and the whole passage turns into a white silt-out. | Sea Firefly | Crustacean | Bioluminescent ostracods (*Vargula*), tiny crustaceans that spit blue light | 1 to 3 mm, swarm 10 cm to 1 m | Skittish (flash blue when disturbed) | Yes, brightness 3 (as a swarm) | Uncommon | Catch a swarm in the lantern and the whole group cheers. Shaking the lantern makes them flash brighter for a moment. |
| Giant Isopod | Crustacean | *Bathynomus giganteus*, a deep-sea scavenger isopod (a new species was found off the Yucatan) | 20 to 50 cm | Harmless, but scavenges bait and dropped gear | No | Uncommon | Rolls slowly toward an injured diver. Not dangerous, just deeply unsettling. Steals a dropped flashlight. |
| Volt Eel | Fish | *Electrophorus voltai*, the electric eel that holds the animal voltage record (860 volts). | Cave Snail Colony | Mollusk | Tiny hydrobiid cave snails | 1 to 5 mm | Harmless | No | Common | Needs the magnifier to measure. Someone always says "it's 0 cm." |
| Siltbloom Puffer | Fish (invented) | Pufferfish plus cave silt camouflage | 10 to 25 cm (60 cm puffed) | Skittish (puffs and blasts silt when startled) | No | Uncommon | Looks like a rock until you touch it, then becomes a silt bomb. |

## Fossils and Treasure

Dig-up items, mostly in silt floors of Zones 3 to 5. Values are relative ($ low, $$$$$ highest). Inspired by real finds in Hoyo Negro, a flooded Yucatan pit that held Ice Age animals (gomphotheres, ground sloths, saber-toothed cats, short-faced bears).

| Cave bear tooth | Zone 3 dry chambers | $$ | Heavy, slightly slows swimming. |
| Ground sloth claw | Zones 3 to 4 | $$$ | Long and silly, players poke each other with it. |
| Saber-tooth fang | Zone 4 | $$$$ | Breaks if dropped. |

## Design rules (keeping logging from getting repetitive)

1. **Variants.** Each species has 2 to 4 variants (size class, color morph, albino, scarred, juvenile). A variant counts as a new logbook line, not a new species.
2. **Shiny rare spawns.** About 1 in 200 spawns is a "shiny" (gold, glowing, or giant). Worth bonus currency and bragging.
3. **Measure records.** The logbook keeps your biggest and smallest measurement for each species, so "I found a bigger olm" is always possible. Hub leaderboards can show these (see [[Progression, Hub and Leaderboards]]).
4. **Behavior entries.** Each species has 1 to 3 hidden behaviors to witness and log (the glowworm dimming, the Slimewarden sliming, the Echo Ray mimicking). Seeing a behavior fills a line in the logbook.
5. **Hard to measure on purpose.** Skittish animals need teamwork (one herds, one measures). Dangerous ones need photos from a distance instead.
6. **Zone mixing is rare, not never.** About 5% of spawns can come from one zone deeper, so shallow runs still have surprises.
7. **Conditions change the spawns.** Warm areas, currents, silt rooms, and air pockets each favor different species. Two caves at the same depth should not feel the same (ties into [[Cave Generation]]).
8. **Don't make everything loggable at once.** Some species only appear after an event (a cave-in reveals a fossil bed; a dead Lanternjaw attracts isopods).
9. **Lantern mixing.** Different glowers mix colors in the lantern (blue ostracods plus green fungus equals teal). Gives players a reason to catch species they already logged.
10. **Funny first, horror second.** Each zone gets at most one or two horror creatures. Everything else creates comedy (rope chaos, slime, burps, cricket swarms).
