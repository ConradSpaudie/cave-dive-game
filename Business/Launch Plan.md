# Launch Plan

How the game goes from "nobody knows it exists" to a free launch on Steam, stage by stage, tied to the [[Roadmap]]. Written 2026-10-17 as a proposal. Working title used below: **Tied Under** (see [[Proposed Answers]] #52). Studio name: **TyLoCon** (decided after #53; Bad Buoyancy was the earlier pick). Swap them if those change.

Related: [[Personal Brand and Marketing]], [[Content Calendar]], [[Monetization]], [[Game Pillars]], [[Research So Far]].

## The short version
1. **Post from day one** (see [[Content Calendar]]). The audience you build before the Steam page exists is the only audience you will have when it goes live.
2. **Steam page goes live at the end of Phase 2** (target: mid January 2027), the moment two people on a rope looks funny on video. Not earlier, not later.
3. **Discord opens the same day as the Steam page.**
4. **Free Steam Playtest at Phase 5** (target: late spring 2027) to test with strangers and gather clips.
5. **One shot at Steam Next Fest: October 2027**, with a demo that streamers can play in under an hour.
6. **Free launch 3 to 5 weeks after Next Fest.** Streamers get early access through Playtest keys.

If the Roadmap slips, slide every date by the same amount. The one rule that never moves: **do not enter Next Fest until strangers laugh while playing the demo.** You only get one Next Fest per game.

## What made PEAK spread (and what to copy)

PEAK (Aggro Crab and Landfall, June 16, 2025) started as a month-long jam-style sprint in Seoul, South Korea (then a few more months of work), and its teaser and Steam page went live only about 4 days before launch. It launched with 29,384 wishlists, then sold about 100,000 copies on day one, over 1 million in the first week, 4.5 million in under a month, and over 10 million by mid-August 2025. There was no big media campaign: it spread through streamers and clips on TikTok and YouTube.

| What happened | Why it worked | Copy it? | How we copy it |
|---|---|---|---|
| Wobbly physics, comical falls, ghost mode after death | Every session makes a clip without anyone trying | **Yes, this is the whole game** | The five funny moments in [[Proposed Answers]] (rope yank, silt blame, squeeze, buoyancy launch, muffled scream) and the ghost bubble on death |
| Proximity voice chat | The funny part is the screaming, not the graphics | **Yes** | Muffled underwater voice that goes crystal clear in air pockets. Ship this in Phase 2. |
| One stamina bar runs the core loop | Simple rule, instantly readable on stream | **Yes** | Air is our one bar. Viewers must understand it in 2 seconds. |
| "What you do" plus "who you are" hook (climb a mountain, lost scouts) | Easy to explain in one sentence | **Yes** | "Broke dive-school dropouts, tied together by one rope, mapping a cave nobody else will dive" |
| Cheap price ($8, $5 launch week) | No friction for a whole friend group to buy | **Better: we're free** | Free means a group of 4 never has to convince the 4th friend to pay. Content Warning (Landfall) went free for its first 24 hours, gave away about 6 million copies and still sold 700,000+ in the week after. |
| Procedural mountain that changes daily | Streamers can play it again and again | **Yes** | New procedural cave every run (Pillar 4) |
| Steam page only 4 days before launch | Landfall and Aggro Crab already had big audiences | **No** | We have no audience. We need months of wishlists and followers first. |
| Influencers playing and having fun was "a giant part of the sales surge" | Viewers watch, then want to play with their own friends | **Yes** | Streamer outreach plan below, and Conrad plays his own game on camera with friends |
| Kept a "game jam" rough feel | Charm beats polish in this genre | **Partly** | Our hook is photoreal (Pillar 2), but the physics and chaos should stay goofy |

Other friendslop hits reportedly point the same way. Super Battle Golf ($8) reportedly passed 500,000 units in 9 days on the back of streamers who came back 5 to 6 times. Roadside Research's demo reportedly pulled 903,000 players with a median playtime of 1h12m. The lesson every time: **an instantly understandable, stream-friendly hook beats a deep game with a complicated pitch.** And the genre isn't full: combined friendslop players reportedly hit about 366,000 in June 2026, and games well below the top spot still sell hundreds of thousands of copies.

## Stage A: Build in public (now to Phase 2)
- Follow the [[Content Calendar]]. At least 1 devlog post a week (3 when you can), same video on Instagram Reels and TikTok.
- Grab the handles now, even before the name is final: Instagram, TikTok, YouTube (for Shorts and longer devlogs), and a dedicated Gmail for the studio. Use the same handle everywhere.
- Every post's call to action is "follow" until the Steam page exists. After that, it's "wishlist, link in bio."
- Save every funny clip from Phase 2 testing with Tyrus. These become the teaser.

## Stage B: Steam page (end of Phase 2, mid January 2027)
Zukowski's advice is to get a page up as early as possible because wishlists don't really age, but only once the art style is settled and visible. For us, that's when the photoreal tunnel and the rope co-op both exist on video, which is the end of Phase 2.

**Before you pay:**
- Steamworks needs the legal name, bank account and tax details of whoever signs the Steam Distribution Agreement. Valve's docs don't state a minimum age, but contracts and bank accounts usually need someone 18+, so plan on a parent setting up the Steamworks account with you.
- Pay the **$100 Steam Direct fee** (the only real cost in this plan). It's paid back to you once the game earns $1,000 in Steam revenue, which for a free game means cosmetic sales.
- Leave about a month between paying and wanting the page public: the store page and later builds are reviewed by Valve.

**Steam page checklist:**

| Item | What it needs |
|---|---|
| Capsule art | Readable at thumbnail size: title "Tied Under" big, one diver, one rope, darkness. Make it from a real in-game screenshot. |
| Short description | One sentence: "A free first-person co-op cave diving game for 1 to 4 friends, all tied to one rope." |
| Teaser trailer | First 5 seconds are gameplay, not logos. See trailer plan below. |
| Screenshots | 5 or more, real gameplay, at least 3 different-looking spots (tunnel, air pocket, dry chamber) so it doesn't look like a one-room game |
| Tags | Co-op, Online Co-Op, Underwater, Exploration, Funny, Physics, Proximity Voice Chat, Procedural Generation, Free to Play, First-Person, Horror (light) |
| AI disclosure | Steam asks you to disclose AI use in the content survey. Answer honestly: AI helps write code. Say so in devlogs too. |
| Links | Discord, Instagram, TikTok |

## Stage C: Grow the page (Phase 3 to 4)
- Keep the weekly devlog cadence. Each Roadmap milestone (first procedural cave, first creature, first air pocket) is a "big" post with "wishlist" as the CTA.
- Post a Steam event or announcement for each big update so followers get notified.
- Use **Steam UTM links** in your bio so you can see which platform brings wishlists.
- Try **Curator Connect** (free, in Steamworks) once the Playtest exists: send keys to curators who cover co-op and horror.

**Wishlist checkpoints:**

| Checkpoint | Goal | If you're behind |
|---|---|---|
| 1 month after page live | 500 | Your posts aren't pointing to Steam. Add the CTA on screen, not only in the caption. |
| End of Phase 4 | 2,000 | Make the funniest rope clip yet and push it everywhere (Reddit r/IndieDev, r/indiegaming, r/coopgaming too) |
| Playtest end | 5,000 | Start streamer outreach early with Playtest keys |
| Next Fest start | 7,000 | Fine to enter anyway, but launch later and do a second push |
| Launch | 15,000+ | Delay launch by a month and run more creator outreach |

Why 7,000: community tracking puts the Popular Upcoming entry bar near 7,000 wishlists. These numbers come from paid-game benchmarks. A free game converts wishlists to players more easily, so hitting them is a good sign.

## Stage D: Steam Playtest (Phase 5)
- Steam Playtest is free for developers and players, lives as a separate app attached to your store page, and doesn't touch your reviews or wishlists. It can't be monetized.
- **Run it in two waves:**
  1. **Limited signup** (2 to 3 weeks): grant access in small batches, mostly Discord members. Fix the worst bugs.
  2. **Open signup** (rest of the summer): anyone who clicks "Request Access" gets in.
- Watch the median playtime. Aim for 45 minutes or more before Next Fest.
- Ask every tester for clips. Repost the best ones (with credit) as content.
- This is when you need the Windows PC for builds (see [[Proposed Answers]] #3).

## Stage E: Steam Next Fest (October 2027)
- **Rules:** a game can join only one Next Fest, the store page must be public, a playable demo must be live when the fest starts, and the game can't release before the fest ends.
- **Why October 2027 and not June 2027:** June 2027's fest runs June 14 to 21; registration reportedly closes around April 25, 2027 and the demo is due by late May, which is the middle of Phase 4. Too early. October 2027 dates aren't announced yet. Based on October 2026 (registration closed August 31, demo and page due September 14), plan for **registration by late August 2027 and a finished demo by early September 2027.**
- **The demo:** yes, even though the full game is free, because Next Fest only features demos. Make it the first two depth zones with a fixed cave seed, 30 to 60 minutes long, ending on a cliffhanger (the first glimpse of something huge below). Streamers prefer a 30 to 90 minute slice.
- **During the fest:** livestream yourself playing with friends every day, reply to every Discord message, post a daily Reel or TikTok with the best stranger clip.

## Stage F: Launch
- Launch 3 to 5 weeks after Next Fest while the wishlists are fresh.
- The game is free, so cosmetics and the Supporter Pack go live at launch (see [[Monetization]]).
- Steam requires a Coming Soon page to be up at least 2 weeks before release, which is already handled because the page went live in January.

## Discord
Opens the same day as the Steam page. Keep it tiny and personal.

| Channel | Purpose |
|---|---|
| #announcements | Devlog drops, Playtest waves, Next Fest |
| #clips | Testers post funny moments. Best ones get featured on Instagram and TikTok. |
| #bugs | Playtest reports (Tyrus owns this) |
| #ideas | Creature and gear suggestions |
| #general | Hang out |

- Role for the first 100 members ("First Divers"). Later, put their names on a wall in the dive camp hub.
- Turn on slow mode and keep the server's DMs off for members you don't know.
- No paid promotion, no giveaways that ask for personal info.

## Trailers

| Trailer | When | Length | Structure |
|---|---|---|---|
| Teaser | Steam page launch (end of Phase 2) | 30 to 45 sec | 0 to 5 sec: flashlight cuts through black water. 5 to 20 sec: rope yank or buoyancy launch with real voice chat. 20 to 35 sec: something moves in the dark. End: title and "Wishlist on Steam." |
| Gameplay trailer | Playtest start (Phase 5) | 60 to 90 sec | Hook moment first, then "1 to 4 friends, one rope," air meter, silt-out, squeeze, creature, the descent. End: "Free. Play the Playtest now." |
| Next Fest trailer | Demo release (September 2027) | 60 sec | Same as gameplay trailer, recut with the best stranger and streamer moments. End: "Play the free demo." |
| Launch trailer | Launch day | 60 to 90 sec | Biggest moments plus the bottom chamber reveal. End: "Free on Steam now." |

Rules for every trailer: real in-game footage, no CGI. Gameplay in the first 5 seconds. Keep proximity voice audio in. Captions for anyone watching on mute. Edit in CapCut or DaVinci Resolve (both free).

## Streamer and creator outreach

**Who to target:** creators who already play PEAK, R.E.P.O., Content Warning, Lethal Company and other friendslop games.
- Twitch: channels averaging 50 to 2,000 viewers (find them on SullyGnome).
- YouTube: "first look" and co-op creators with about 10K to 500K subscribers.
- TikTok: gaming clip accounts that post friendslop moments.
- Don't skip small channels. A mid-sized channel can bring 1,000 wishlists in two days and pull bigger creators in after.

**Tools (all free or have free options):**

| Tool | What it does | When to use it |
|---|---|---|
| Steam Curator Connect | Send keys to up to 100 Steam curators, filtered by tags | Playtest and launch |
| Keymailer | Creators request keys, you approve based on their stats (free tier) | Playtest onward |
| Woovit | Creators opt into your campaign, stricter verification (freemium) | Next Fest and launch |
| Lurkit | Influencer campaign platform with an indie developer program | Check at Next Fest time |
| Direct email | Your own list of 100+ creators in a spreadsheet | The main tool. Start building the list during Stage C. |

**Numbers to plan around:** for about 10 videos, contact 100+ creators directly plus run an open campaign. One developer's month on Woovit reached 95 creators and got 69 videos. Another sent 30 cold emails and got zero replies at first, then a video two months later. Expect slow replies.

**Timing:** contact your whole list three times: Playtest open, 2 to 4 weeks before Next Fest (with the demo), and 1 to 2 weeks before launch (with Playtest keys for early play).

**Email template (under 150 words):**

> Subject: Free co-op cave diving game for your channel (demo link inside)
>
> Hi [name], I loved your [specific video] where [specific funny moment].
>
> I'm Conrad, I'm building Tied Under: a free first-person co-op cave diving game where 1 to 4 friends are tied together by one rope. Currents drag the whole team, someone always kicks up silt, and voices go muffled underwater.
>
> Demo: [Steam link]. Trailer: [link]. GIF attached.
>
> No embargo, no strings. Play it whenever.
>
> Conrad, TyLoCon

Write the first line for real every time. Never send the same copy to everyone.

**Bonus angle:** "Teen builds photoreal game" is a story for gaming news and local press. Pitch it once the Playtest is live.

## Press kit
Make it once at Steam page launch, update it for Next Fest. Host it free (presskit.gg, a Notion page, or a page on the studio site). Put the link in every email.

| Section | Contents |
|---|---|
| Fact sheet | Game name, studio, developers (first names only), platform (Steam, Windows), price (Free), release target, player count (1 to 4), Steam link, contact email |
| Description | One-sentence pitch, one short paragraph, 5 bullet features |
| Trailers | YouTube links plus downloadable MP4s |
| Screenshots | 8 to 10 at 1920x1080, no HUD clutter |
| GIFs | 3 to 5 short loops of the funny moments |
| Logos | Game logo and studio logo, PNG on transparent background |
| Key art | Capsule art in large size |
| About the team | Two teens built it with AI coding help. No school, age, or city details. |
| Contact | Studio Gmail only. Never a personal email or phone number. |
