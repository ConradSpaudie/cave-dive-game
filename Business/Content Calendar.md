# Content Calendar (first 12 weeks)

The first 12 weeks of Instagram Reels and TikTok, starting from zero followers and zero footage. Lines up with [[Roadmap]] Phases 0 to 2 and with Stage A of the [[Launch Plan]]. Proposal written 2026-10-17.

Related: [[Personal Brand and Marketing]], [[Launch Plan]], [[Game Pillars]], [[Proposed Answers]].

## The big idea
Weeks 1 to 3 have no game to show, so **the story is the content**: a teen with no game dev experience trying to build a photoreal co-op game with AI. People follow a person and a goal before they follow a game. From week 4 the footage takes over, and from week 7 the rope co-op chaos with Tyrus becomes the star.

## Content pillars

| Pillar | What it is | Share of posts |
|---|---|---|
| 1. The challenge | "Can a teen with no experience pull this off?" Progress, setbacks, honest updates | 30% |
| 2. Look at this | Short, satisfying game footage: light in dark water, silt clouds, bubbles | 30% |
| 3. Friends and chaos | Conrad and Tyrus (and friends) playing, failing, screaming | 25% |
| 4. Real cave diving | Quick facts about real cave diving that the game uses (silt-outs, buoyancy, line rules) | 15% |

## Posting cadence
- **Minimum 1 devlog post a week** (the Tuesday slot), as in [[Proposed Answers]] #54. **Stretch goal: 3 posts a week (Tuesday, Thursday, Sunday)** using the extra posts below when you have time. Same video goes to TikTok and Instagram Reels the same day.
- Post in the evening, Pacific time (6 to 8 pm). Check your own analytics after week 4 and move it if your followers are online at another time.
- **1 story a day on Instagram** is a bonus, not required: a screenshot, a poll, a "working on it" clip.
- Budget about **2 hours a week** on content, separate from the 10 hours of building: film during dev sessions, edit everything in one sitting on Saturday.
- Consistency beats volume. Never post daily for two weeks then vanish. If a week is bad, post 2 and keep going.

## Simple editing workflow

| Step | Tool (free) | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Capture | OBS Studio (Mac screen and gameplay), iPhone camera | Record every dev session. Keep OBS running while you test. |
| 2. Dump | One folder per week: `Week 07/raw` | Move clips from phone and Mac on Saturday |
| 3. Pick | Your eyes | Watch only the moments you marked. Pick 3. |
| 4. Cut | **CapCut** (desktop or phone) | Cut to the hook first. Remove every pause. Aim for under 30 sec. |
| 5. Captions | CapCut auto captions | Always on. Most people watch muted. Fix the words it gets wrong. |
| 6. Sound | Platform sound library only | Use in-app sounds for music (licensed). Keep game audio and voice loud. |
| 7. Export | 1080x1920, vertical | One export, upload to both TikTok and Instagram. Remove any watermark before cross-posting. |
| 8. Post | TikTok and Instagram apps | Same day, same caption. Add 3 to 5 tags: #gamedev #indiedev #unrealengine5 #coopgames #cavediving |

Rules for every video:
- The first frame must already be interesting. No "hey guys," no logo intro.
- Put the hook as text on screen too, in the top third.
- When a video has a person, cut every 2 to 3 seconds. Gameplay loops can be one long shot.
- Before the Steam page exists, save the best clips in a "trailer" folder.

## Talking on camera

| Tip | Why |
|---|---|
| Say the hook first, then explain | You have 2 seconds before people scroll |
| Film 3 takes of the hook, keep the best | The hook matters more than the rest of the video |
| Hold the phone at eye level, window light in front of you | Looks good without buying anything |
| Talk to one friend, not "everyone" | Sounds natural instead of like a presentation |
| Use a 5-word note card, not a script | Scripts make you sound like you're reading |
| Talk a little faster and louder than normal | Camera flattens energy |
| Cut the "ums" in editing, don't stress about them while filming | Jump cuts are normal on short-form |
| Film at least one talking video every week | This is the skill you said you want. Reps are the only way. |
| Watch your own video once at full speed before posting | You'll catch the boring part immediately |

Speech and debate helps here: treat every video like a 30-second opening with one claim and one piece of proof.

## Metrics to track
Log these every Sunday in one table (a note in this vault or a spreadsheet). Check them once a week, not every hour.

| Metric | Where | What "good" looks like early on |
|---|---|---|
| Views per post | TikTok and Instagram analytics | Most organic posts get under 1,000. One breakout a month is a win. |
| Average watch time / % watched | Analytics per post | Over 50% on a 20-second clip |
| 3-second hold (how many stay past the hook) | Instagram "skip rate," TikTok retention graph | Improving week over week |
| Shares and saves | Analytics | Shares matter more than likes. These are the clips to remake. |
| Follows per post | Analytics | Track which pillar brings followers |
| Comments | Analytics | Engagement (likes plus comments divided by views) of 5 to 15% is strong |
| Profile visits and link clicks | Analytics | Starts mattering in week 12 |
| Wishlists | Steamworks, using a UTM link per platform | From week 12 on. A strong post adds 50 to 500. |

Every 4 weeks, ask: which pillar got the most follows? Make more of that next month.

## Posting safely as a teen
Short and practical. Do these once and forget about them.
- **Never show:** your school name, uniform or logo, your street, your house from outside, car plates, or anything that pins down which city you're in right now. Check the background of every shot.
- **Post after, not during:** if you film somewhere outside home, post it later, not live.
- **Friends:** ask before posting anyone, and if they're under 18, make sure their parents are OK with it.
- **Platform rules:** TikTok LIVE and gifts are 18+. Instagram Teen Accounts are private by default and only let people you follow message you. If you're under 16, a parent has to approve switching to a public account. Turn on public for the brand account with a parent's OK.
- **DMs:** keep them off for strangers. Business contact goes to the studio Gmail. Never move a conversation to another app with someone you don't know.
- **Money and deals:** any brand deal, sponsorship, or payment goes through a parent.
- **AI honesty:** say clearly that AI helps you write code. Gamers react badly when it comes out later. Being upfront is part of the story.
- **Music and clips:** use the platform's sound library, and credit any outside clip (PEAK footage, real cave diving videos).
